Let $\PP^2$ be a projetive plane 
with homogeneous coordinates $[x:y:z]$, and 
let $\gamma$ be the involution of $\PP^2$ given by
\[
[x:y:z]\mapsto [x:y:-z].
\]
Then a plane sextic  is  $\gamma$-invariant if and only if it is defined by an equation 
of the form 
\[
f_{6}(x, y)+f_{4}(x, y)z^2+ f_{2}(x, y) z^4+ f_{0}(x, y) z^6=0,
\]
where $f_{\nu} (x, y)$ is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $\nu$.
Hence the family $\FFF$  of $\gamma$-invariant sextics is parametrized 
by a $15$-dimensional projective space.
Note that a general member $C\subset \PP^2$ of $\FFF$ is non-singular.
%
%
\begin{proposition}
Let $C$ be a  general member of $\FFF$,
and let $\XC\to \PP^2$ be the double plane branching along $C$.
\begin{enumerate}[label={\rm (\arabic*)}]
\item The plane sextic  $C$ has exactly $120$ six-tangential conics.
\item The $K3$ surface $\XC$ has exactly $240$ smooth rational curves,
which are pull-backs of the six-tangential conics of $C$.
\item The Picard lattice of $\XC$ is of rank $9$
with a Gram matrix 
\[
\diag (2, -2,-2,-2,-2,-2,-2,-2,-2).
\]
The nef-and-big cone of $\XC$ is a polyhedron with finitely many faces, 
and these faces are defined by the $240$ smooth rational curves.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
